Voice-acting collages

A dataset of short performances stitched from many takes by the same voice, arranged so the emotion moves across the clip instead of sitting still. This page measures what source material exists, prices three build scales, writes the procedural rules a builder would implement, and recommends one design.
Written 2026-08-16 on JUPITER. Every number is marked measured or estimated; measured ones say which file they came from.

The 60-second version Take 3–6 short clips spoken by one voice, trim the silence off their ends, glue them together, and you get a single 40-second performance that starts angry, cools into resentment, sinks into sadness and ends with a rueful half-laugh. Nothing in that arc was ever recorded as one performance — it is assembled. The dataset is millions of such assemblies, each re-annotated as a single item and each carrying a written direction describing how the delivery should move.

The hard part is not volume. 1.29 × 1023 distinct collages are combinatorially available from our synthetic corpus alone; picking a big number and generating it would produce a dataset in which every underlying clip is reused hundreds of times. The hard part is coverage — sampling across trajectory shapes — and the hard constraint is that the speaker-similarity gate we would naturally use to keep seams clean throws away the exact material the dataset is about.

1 · What is being built

Almost all speech training data is flat: one utterance, one emotional state, held for the length of the clip. Real acting is not like that. A performance moves — a line begins in anger and ends in something quieter; amusement curdles into bitterness over four sentences. A model trained only on flat clips has never seen the transition, only the endpoints.

A collage is a synthetic performance built by concatenation:

clip 1 Anger, full intensity
"Are you serious with this?"
clip 2 Anger, contained
irritation tucked under the words
clip 3 Bitterness
"an exhausting climb"
clip 4 Sadness + amusement
a rueful half-laugh

All four clips are spoken by the same voice. They were generated independently, seconds apart, as answers to four different prompts. Trimmed and joined they read as one continuous take with a moving emotional line. The finished 40-second item is then re-annotated from scratch — VoiceNet's 57 acoustic dimensions, Empathic Insight's 40 emotion intensities, a speaker embedding, a caption — and that annotation describes the arc, not any one clip.

Five constraints define the whole design, and they are in tension with each other:

  1. Neighbouring clips must sound like the same person. A seam where the voice changes is a defect, not a performance.
  2. Emotion must change, but continuously. Two or three distinct emotional states across a collage, reached in gradual steps — not a cut from ecstasy to grief.
  3. Vocal bursts are an explicit exception. A scream, a sob or a burst of laughter dominates the speaker embedding: the same person screaming does not embed like the same person talking. A seam that lands on a burst is allowed to break the similarity rule. This is written as a rule with its own threshold, not waved through case by case.
  4. Silence is trimmed at every clip head and tail before joining, so the seams do not gap.
  5. The finished collage is re-annotated, and gets one caption describing the whole arc.

Recommend: ingest it into the annotated corpus for the German TTS tier, exclude it from collage v1.

5 · The seam problem — this is the central design decision

The natural rule is "require high speaker similarity at every seam". Applied to this corpus it is close to self-defeating, and the reason is measurable.

5.2 The floor is probably miscalibrated — and the embedder is weaker than assumed

Two independent lines of evidence say the 0.40 floor is not where it should be.

(a) An independent WavLM speaker embedder puts 75.4 % of the takes that fail the ECAPA floor above its own same-speaker threshold. Two embedders disagreeing on three quarters of the rejections is not a marginal calibration issue.

A gate at 0.40 rejects 61 % of genuinely same-voice pairs to avoid 4.8 % of genuinely different-voice pairs — and since collages are built within one voice by construction, the different-voice error rate is not a risk we are actually exposed to. Two caveats, stated so nobody over-reads this. First, all 500 "voices" are synthesised by the same model from reference conditioning, so they are more similar to each other than 500 real humans would be — the false-accept column is pessimistic and the EER is not a claim about ECAPA in general. Second, emotion itself perturbs the embedding, which is the whole point: the "same-voice" spread is wide because the takes differ in emotion, exactly as the burst exception predicts at a smaller scale.

5.3 The decision

Do not gate on spk_sim to the reference at all. Gate on clip-to-clip similarity at the seam, at a threshold set from the measured same-voice distribution, with an explicit burst exemption. Concretely, and with the reasoning for each number:

  • Seam threshold 0.30 on clip-to-clip ECAPA cosine, not 0.40 and not to the reference. 0.30 is the 25th percentile of the same-voice distribution (p25 = 0.230, median 0.372) and still sits above the p95 of the different-voice control (0.351) by enough to be meaningful at 5.7× enrichment. It accepts 56.9 % of same-voice pairs instead of 38.7 %.
  • Confirm with a second embedder. Accept a seam if either ECAPA ≥ 0.30 or WavLM is above its own same-speaker threshold. Given that WavLM rescues 75.4 % of ECAPA's rejections, this is the single change that recovers the most emotional material for the least risk.
  • Never compare across voices. The voice identity is known from the source metadata; the embedding check is a defect detector (did this take come out sounding like a different person because the emotion broke the synthesis?), not an identity check. Framing it that way is what justifies the lower threshold.

Within one class the drop is real but modest — about 0.08 in the median. The extreme cases are in the tail: ga_amuse_laugh sits at a mean spk_sim of 0.257 with 76.6 % below the floor, and the sports block (shouting) at 0.273 / 80.2 %. So the exception should be class-specific, not a blanket pass:

BURST_EXEMPT_CLASSES = {          # from the edge/burst_isolated blocks
    "fear_scream", "pain_scream", "pain_groan", "amused_laughter",
    "ga_amuse_laugh", "sad_crying", "tears", "whimpering",
    "cold_shiver", "ga_*"                      # ga_ = "goes all the way" variants
}

def seam_ok(a, b):
    cos = ecapa_cos(a, b)
    if cos >= 0.30:            return True          # normal seam
    if wavlm_same_speaker(a,b): return True         # second-opinion rescue
    # burst exemption: at most ONE side may be a burst, and the burst must be
    # confidently classified, not a low-energy false positive
    burst_side = (a.burst_class in BURST_EXEMPT_CLASSES) ^ (b.burst_class in BURST_EXEMPT_CLASSES)
    if burst_side and max(a.blend, b.blend) >= 4.0 and cos >= 0.10:
        return True                                  # exempt seam, FLAGGED in metadata
    return False

Three things about this rule are deliberate:

  • Exclusive-or, not or. Two bursts either side of a seam gives no anchor at all — the seam becomes unverifiable. At most one side may be exempt.
  • A blend floor of 4.0/10 on the burst side. The burst locator has a known false-positive problem: in the smoke test, 77 of 151 detected spans came back as "Low Mumble" and 39 as "Ahem", which looks like the locator firing on low-energy speech rather than on bursts. Requiring a strong vocal-burst blend score means the exemption is only granted to something that actually sounds like a burst. In the quality-selected pool the mean blend is 4.15 at top-5, so this floor is roughly the median of the selected material, not an exotic requirement.
  • A residual floor of 0.10 even for exempt seams, which is still above the different-voice median of 0.095. An exempt seam is allowed to be bad; it is not allowed to be indistinguishable from a different person.
  • Every exempt seam is recorded in the collage metadata as seam[i].exempt = true with the class and the cosine. Downstream consumers can filter them out; a human evaluator can listen to exactly those. Never let an exemption be invisible.

6 · Can we even measure the emotion? — a result that changes the design

The whole dataset rests on being able to say that clip 2 is emotionally between clip 1 and clip 3. So I checked whether the per-clip emotion read-out actually supports that. It largely does not, and this is important enough to change how the arcs get built.

Method: parse the 40-dimensional Empathic-Insight vector for every clip in the quality pool of 8 voices (33,670 clips), compute the centroid of each of the 40 intended emotions, and ask how often a clip's own emotion is the nearest centroid.

Two more numbers make the same point from different directions. The emo_argmax field agrees with the intended emotion on only 11.3 % of all takes (14.6 % of top-1 takes), and "Interest" is the argmax for 65 % of the whole corpus. And the L2 distance between two clips that were asked for the same emotion has a median of 4.67, versus 4.87 for two clips picked at random — barely distinguishable.

The signal is real but it lives in the average, not in the clip Averaged over ~20 takes the emotion labels are recovered at 90.8 %, and the resulting centroid geometry is semantically excellent — the nearest neighbour of Anger is Impatience (0.93), of Bitterness is Sourness (0.71), of Distress is Helplessness (0.54), of Amusement is Teasing (0.54). That is not noise; that is a correct map of emotional space. But a single clip's 40-dim vector is too noisy to place that clip on the map.

Consequence for the design: build arcs on the intended condition label (which is exact — it is the prompt we asked for) positioned at its measured centroid, and use the per-clip vector only as a soft tie-breaker and as a post-hoc audit statistic. Do not compute "emotion delta per step" from raw per-clip vectors; the noise is larger than the signal you are trying to control.

6.1 The emotional plane, measured

A PCA of the 40 emotion centroids gives two interpretable axes that together carry 41.0 % of the variance (PC1 23.8 %, PC2 17.2 %):

  • PC1 — valence. Negative loadings: Distress (−0.43), Sadness (−0.38), Helplessness (−0.34), Disappointment (−0.32). Positive: Interest (+0.25), Hope (+0.24), Elation (+0.19).
  • PC2 — hostility. Positive: Impatience (+0.40), Anger (+0.30), Contempt (+0.25), Sourness (+0.22). Negative: Thankfulness (−0.28), Hope (−0.26), Relief (−0.26), Affection (−0.22).

The plane is for reading and for checking direction of travel. Distances should be computed in the full 40-dimensional centroid space, because the two components carry only 41 % of the variance and some perceptually large moves collapse in the projection — Distress → Emotional Numbness is 1.37 in 40-D but only 0.20 in the plane, because the plane has no arousal axis and both sit in the negative-valence corner. Everything below is 40-D measured: minimum centroid separation 0.536, p10 1.219, median 2.027, p90 2.960, maximum 4.280 (Anger ↔ Thankfulness).

Step radius 1.3 is the operating point. 7 · Natural corpora: how many speakers actually qualify

The raw utterance counts in section 2 are not what the natural corpora contribute. A collage needs one speaker with enough clips and enough emotional range. A speaker with 200 flat clips yields no arc. So the number that matters is how many speakers pass all three tests.

This section is exact, not sampled Because the completed annotation run wrote a per-utterance index parquet for every shard, the whole question could be answered by reading all 13,098 index parquets — every clip of emolia, podcast, eurospeech and mls — on the login node in about 90 seconds per dataset with column-pruned reads. So the speaker counts, clip distributions and emotion statistics below are measured over the complete corpus, not extrapolated. Only Common Voice is estimated: its speaker id does not appear in the index parquet at all and had to be recovered from inside the data tars, so it was measured on 8 German tars (58,629 clips) and scaled ×10.37 to the 83 German shards.

7.1 First: does the speaker key mean anything?

None of these corpora carries a speaker column in the index. Identity has to be derived from the uid, and a derived key is worthless if it does not group one voice. The test: pool the within-key variance of a VoiceNet dimension that should be constant for a person and divide by the total variance. Near 0 means the key isolates a voice; near 1 means it groups a mixture. A shuffled-label control gives the "no information" reference.

Three findings here are worth stating separately, because each one changes what a builder would do.

  • Emilia has two uid schemes and the obvious rule breaks on half of it. Audiobook-style uids look like DE_B00000_S00000_W000000 (B = tar bucket — one bucket holds ~6,000 speakers, so B alone is not a speaker; S = speaker index within it). YODAS-style uids look like DE_<youtube_id>_W000000, and YouTube ids frequently contain underscores (DE_TU-Z_DR-vYM_W000000). Splitting on the first three fields returns the entire uid for those, i.e. one clip per "speaker", silently destroying ~54 % of the corpus. The correct universal rule is strip the trailing _W######.
  • EuroParl has no speaker identity anywhere. Confirmed by extracting the per-utterance JSON out of the output tars: src_meta carries country, video id, transcript id, start/end seconds, CER and WER — nothing speaker-like. The keys are parliamentary sessions containing many MEPs, which is exactly what the 0.854 variance ratio says.
  • Common Voice speaker ids are recoverable — cv_client_id, cv_age and cv_gender survive inside src_meta in the per-utterance JSON in the data tars; they are merely absent from the index parquet. No join back to the original Common Voice release is needed. This should be promoted into the index.
EuroParl's 7.7 M utterances contribute zero collages as things stand Without a speaker id there is no way to build a same-speaker collage from it. The fix is available but is a project of its own: cluster the VoiceCLAP embeddings the pipeline already wrote next to every clip (*.vclap.npy inside the output tars) into per-session speaker identities. The same applies to Kartoffelphon, whose diarization labels are episode-scoped. Neither should be assumed into any plan until that clustering exists and has been checked.

7.6 Other things the full pass turned up

  • Expressiveness differs enormously by source. Mean genuineness_0_6: podcast 3.97, Emilia and EuroParl in between, MLS 1.05, Common Voice 0.95. The read-aloud corpora are flat-affect by this measure, and the single most expressive body of natural material we have is the one that cannot be released.
  • No emotion head is constant, but several are nearly dead. In EuroParl three heads have std < 0.05 (Infatuation 0.033, Pain 0.036, Sexual Lust 0.044); Pain, Infatuation and Jealousy are low-variance in every corpus. No NaNs anywhere; top_emotion_value spans 0.05–5.65 with no clipping artefact. So the heads are running — some of them just have nothing to detect in parliamentary speech, which is reassuring rather than alarming.
  • MLS has 307 speakers in total — 75 of them German — across 432,177 clips. Enormous clip counts per speaker (max 38,409) and negligible speaker diversity. Eleven speaker ids collide across languages, so it must be keyed by (lang, id) or it will merge two different people.
  • Common Voice clips-per-client tops out at exactly 40 in the shards scanned, and CV's usual power users with hundreds of recordings are absent. Either the shard packing caps them or this subset does; that was not resolved, so the ×10.37 German extrapolation carries that assumption.
  • The Emilia YODAS half needs a per-key voice filter. Its keys are videos, at a 0.239 consistency ratio, so a fraction contain more than one speaker. Dropping keys whose within-key vn_GEND_reg spread exceeds a threshold is cheap and was not applied to the counts above — so the YODAS-derived speaker numbers are an upper bound.
  • Minor: vn_VALE_reg does not exist; the valence heads are vn_VALN_reg and vn_VALS_reg.

7.7 Why v1 should still be synthetic-only

  1. The arcs can be commissioned rather than discovered. In the synthetic corpus we asked for Anger and we know we asked; the label is exact. In Emilia we would have to detect that a speaker moved from anger to sadness using a head that is right 18 % of the time per clip. The same 40-dim vector supports both, but only one of them has ground truth.
  2. Emotional coverage is guaranteed by construction. Every one of the 500 synthetic voices has all 40 emotions at all 4 settings in both languages. In Emilia a speaker's available emotions are whatever they happened to say, and 73 % of it reads as Interest. Filling a 12-archetype × 500-voice coverage matrix is a scheduling problem for the synthetic corpus and a search problem — possibly an infeasible one — for the natural corpora.
  3. Half the synthetic corpus is German (804,976 of 1,609,947 sampled candidates), against 3.2 % of Emilia. For a German-heavy deliverable that ratio is decisive.
  4. The natural corpora are not wasted — they are v2. Emilia alone offers 514,745 qualifying speakers against the synthetic corpus's 500, which is exactly the axis the synthetic corpus is weak on. But EuroParl and the podcast need the VoiceCLAP clustering first, Common Voice needs its cv_client_id promoted into the index, and all of them need an honest answer on the Interest-prior question before anyone builds arcs out of them.

9 · Coverage strategy and trajectory archetypes

Layer 2 — within each archetype, sample across the other axes

  • Voice: uniform over all 500. Never let a voice exceed 1/300 of any archetype's budget.
  • Language: 50/50 DE/EN, matching the corpus. Do not mix languages inside a collage — that is a different research question and it would confound the arc.
  • Length: L ∈ {3,4,5,6} at 30/30/25/15 %. Short arcs are more likely to be clean; long ones carry more transitions per item. Weighted toward the short end because seam risk compounds.
  • Intensity path: the four condition settings A–D are an intensity/containment axis (A and C are the loud ones: 57.3 % and 53.7 % below the old floor; B and D the contained ones at 43.2 % and 42.7 %). Sample intensity trajectories too — rising, falling, and flat — because "the same emotion, held but contained" is itself a transition worth learning.
  • VoiceNet dimensions: 15 % of collages should draw at least one clip from the voicenet block rather than the emotion block, so the dataset contains arcs where the manner changes (tempo, breathiness, brightness) while the emotion holds.

Layer 3 — coverage audit, not volume audit

The release criterion is a filled coverage matrix, not a collage count. Ship when every cell of 12 archetypes × 500 voices × 2 languages × 4 lengths = 48,000 cells has at least 20 collages, which is 960,000 collages minimum and lands naturally near the 1 M scenario. Publish the coverage matrix with the dataset; it is more informative than the row count.

The duration floor is not a detail. Case six. Ranking naively by genuineness + burst blend picks a take shorter than 3 seconds 12.6 % of the time, against a base rate of 2.47 % — a 5.1× enrichment for degenerate takes measured. 4.0 % of top-1 picks are under one second. Inspecting them, the "best" Anger take for one voice is a 0.1-second clip containing the single word "Yeah." (genuineness 2.18/6, blend 6.74/10, spk_sim 0.000); the "best" Amusement take is a 0.9-second "Seriously?". These are truncated generations that score highly because a short breathy fragment reads as both genuine and burst-like.

This project has already produced five separate cases of a scorer confidently rewarding broken audio. This is the sixth, and it was found by looking, not by the scorer flagging it. The existing pipeline score column already guards against it (0.48 % under 3 s), so use composite score or add the floor — but do not rank on genuineness+blend alone.

The fix is nearly free: a 4-second floor removes 3.9 % of candidates and 99.99 % of groups still retain ≥ 5 survivors. It also raises the selected pool's mean duration from 9.03 s to 10.09 s and its mean spk_sim from 0.359 to 0.385.

METHOD    energy-based, 20 ms frames, RMS in dB
REF       the clip's own 95th-percentile frame level  (not a global constant — clip
          loudness varies, see 10.4)
THRESHOLD ref - 35 dB, clamped to a floor of -60 dBFS
TRIM      cut to the first and last frame above threshold, then RESTORE 30 ms of the
          original signal at each end as a pad
FALLBACK  if no frame exceeds threshold, the clip is silent -> reject it entirely

The −35 dB relative threshold is chosen because it sits below any voiced speech frame but above room tone and MP3 pre-echo; the 30 ms pad exists so that a plosive or breath onset is not clipped off the front of the word, which is a much more audible artefact than a 30 ms gap. A voice-activity detector (webrtcvad) is the obvious alternative and would be more robust on noisy natural-corpus audio; for our synthetic material, which has no room tone, the energy method is sufficient and 100× cheaper. Use VAD for the natural corpora, energy for the synthetic.

10.3 Joining — butt-join with a micro-fade, not a crossfade

JOIN      butt-join with a 15 ms equal-power fade-out on the outgoing clip and a 15 ms
          fade-in on the incoming one, overlapping — i.e. a 15 ms crossfade, no more
GAP       0 ms between speech. Optionally insert a deliberate pause (see below).

Reasoning: a real crossfade of 50–200 ms would blend two different phonations and produce an audible "doubling" at every seam — a signature artefact that a model would learn as a feature of the data rather than of speech. 15 ms is under one pitch period at 70 Hz and shorter than any phoneme, so it removes the click from the waveform discontinuity without blending content. It is the smallest fade that reliably kills the click at 48 kHz.

Deliberate pauses. An emotional transition in real speech usually has a breath or a beat in it. Insert a pause at a seam with probability 0.5, drawn from N(280 ms, 90 ms) clipped to [120, 600 ms], filled with 40 dB-down room tone rather than digital silence (digital silence between two speech segments is itself an artefact a model can key on). Always insert a pause of at least 250 ms at a burst-exempt seam — a scream does not run straight into a sentence.

Measured across the same 400 clips: RMS level mean −23.20 dBFS, standard deviation 1.78 dB, p05→p95 range 4.65 dB; no clipping (peak > 0.99 in 0/400).

TARGET    normalise each clip to -23 LUFS integrated (EBU R128) BEFORE joining

The ±3 dB window is the compromise between two failure modes. Full normalisation flattens the dynamics and destroys half the emotional information; no normalisation leaves 4.65 dB steps at seams, which is well above the ~1 dB just-noticeable difference and reads as an edit. 3 dB is a factor of two in power — clearly audible as expressive dynamics, not as a mistake.

10b · Text per level — measured, and it does not work everywhere

A trajectory is a ladder: the same voice at rising levels of one dimension, spliced so the progression is audible in one listen. The obvious way to make a ladder legible is to write the condition into the words at each rung, scaled with the level. That was ablated properly — 106 arms × 16 candidates = 1,696 generations, one reference voice, zero failures — and the answer is that it works for some dimensions and actively harms others.

Score a ladder on span and monotonicity, never on maximum. A recipe that pushes all four rungs to "very high" has destroyed the dimension: you can no longer ask for a little of it. This is the metric the ablation used, with word error rate as a guard rail.

Why chunking fails while disfluency succeeds — this predicts the rest. Inserting ellipses makes the model hesitate, which is what the disfluency head measures. It does not make the model restructure the line into distinct breath groups, which is what the chunking head measures. Two dimensions that sound related respond to completely different interventions. Do not assume a text trick transfers between dimensions that merely sound similar — measure each one before adding it to the builder.

Builder consequence. The per-level text treatment is applied to the dimensions where it was measured to help, and withheld where it was measured to hurt. Predicted-but-unmeasured candidates — Intoxication, Grief and Hesitancy at the strongest setting, then Fatigue, Confusion, Embarrassment, Shame, Distress, Fear, Sadness, Helplessness, Awkwardness — are treated as hypotheses, not as settings, until each is ablated. Expect them to work where the condition produces hesitation and to fail where it produces restructuring.

10c · Splicing and voice conversion: convert the assembled clip, not the rungs

A trajectory is spliced, so it inherits the seam problem of §5 — and per-part voice conversion does not solve it. In a production run VC was applied to each part before splicing and the assembled clips still sounded fragmented. The cause is structural: each part is converted with no knowledge of its neighbours, so the converter cannot smooth a seam it never sees, and splicing independently-converted parts can even emphasise the joins.

Measured over 80 parts, per-part VC moved speaker similarity +0.065 mean with 75 % of parts improving — it was working — and the scene was still fragmented. Fixing identity per rung is not the same problem as producing one continuous performance.

Rule: assemble first, then convert the finished clip in one pass against the anchor. Keep both variants, because a single pass can also flatten prosodic changes that were meant to differ across a rung boundary — which in a trajectory is the whole point.

Also worth carrying into cost planning: DNSMOS improved in only 45 % of converted parts. Voice conversion is a repair for identity, not an audio-quality upgrade. Sidon restoration is the separate tool for quality, measured at 20.7× realtime, 48 kHz out.

11 · Captions: one procedural caption, or an LLM-written direction?

Two options were specified. Both are worked below on the same real collage. The component clips are genuine takes from voice emolia_c0039, selected by z(genuineness)+z(blend) within their groups; the procedural captions are the actual caption_gen strings from the parquet, abbreviated only where marked.

Option A — one procedural caption for the whole collage

Re-run the existing procedural captioner on the finished 40-second file. It reads the newly computed VoiceNet buckets and emotion argmax of the concatenation and emits its template:

A The acoustic signature reflects a fully developed, The audio delivers a standard
masculine vocal signature characterized voice; tempo: The speech has a brisk; arousal:
The physiological activation is clearly elevated; warmth: The resonance perfectly
strikes the standard human baseline; brightness: The spectral balance represents the
perfect; reads as interest, impatience and irritability, concentration; style narrator
style, casual style; genuineness 1.4/6, vocal-burst blend 3.1/10; 38.2s, EN.
Spoken: "Are you serious with this? Your dough is clearly too dry ... Honestly,
there's no fancy betting bonus for this candidates tournament, which is just
insulting ... It just hurts to watch how some men carry this unending weight ...
Seriously, the film with its deeply thought-provoking finish actually snagged a
golden globe. I mean, can you believe it?"

What is wrong with it is visible in the third line. "reads as interest, impatience and irritability, concentration" is the argmax over the whole 38 seconds — it describes the average emotion of a clip whose entire purpose is that the emotion is not constant. The arc has been averaged away. Every attribute in that caption is a scalar; a collage's defining property is a vector of change. Option A is cheap (CPU-only, effectively free) and completely faithful to the measurements, and it describes the wrong thing.

Option B — feed the component captions to a small LLM and ask for a direction

Prompt the model with the four component procedural captions in order, plus the arc metadata (labels, conditions, seam pauses), and ask for a single free-form direction of the kind a director gives an actor. Input for this example, abbreviated:

You are a voice director. Below are four takes, in the order they will be heard as one
continuous 38-second performance by one actor. Write ONE short direction (2-4 sentences)
describing how the delivery should MOVE across the whole thing. Describe the movement,
not each clip. Do not invent emotions that are not listed. Do not mention clips, takes,
seams, or editing.

[1] Anger, full intensity. "reads as impatience and irritability, anger, concentration;
    style narrator, storytelling; genuineness 0.3/6, blend 2.2/10; 7.2s"
    Spoken: "Are you serious with this? Your dough is clearly too dry..."

    style conversational, casual; genuineness 2.1/6, blend 1.6/10; 7.4s"

[4] Amusement, contained. "reads as interest, astonishment surprise, amusement; style

The hallucination problem is not hypothetical, and section 6 makes it worse The LLM is given the intended emotion labels and the component captions. It is not given the audio. If the arc is not audible — because the emotion adapters under-fired, because the per-clip emotion recovery is 18 %, or because the takes selected happen to be flat — the model will still write a fluent, confident direction describing a beautiful emotional descent that is not in the audio. Note that in the very example above, three of the four component captions independently report "reads as interest, concentration" — the measured read-out of clips that were asked for anger, bitterness and sadness. An LLM handed those captions plus the label "Anger → Bitterness → Sadness" will describe the labels, not the measurements, because the labels tell a better story.

That is a caption-generator that produces training targets uncorrelated with the audio, which is worse than no caption at all: it teaches a model that a specific direction corresponds to arbitrary delivery.

Recommendation: B, but constrained — and ship A alongside it

Generate both. Ship both. Make the direction structurally unable to invent the arc it is describing.

  1. Emit the procedural caption (Option A) on the finished collage regardless. It costs nothing, it is the ground-truth attribute record, and it is what you check the direction against.
  2. Extend the procedural captioner to be arc-aware before the LLM ever sees it: instead of one argmax over 38 seconds, run the emotion and VoiceNet heads over a sliding window and emit a sequence — "opens at high arousal / hostile; falls through the midpoint; ends low-arousal, warm". This is a small change (the heads are already per-window) and it fixes Option A's actual defect. Feed these measured window statistics to the LLM, not just the component labels.
  3. Constrain the LLM: temperature 0, a pinned Gemma-class 2B/4B, 2–4 sentences, explicitly forbidden to name emotions that do not appear in the measured window sequence, and required to describe direction of change rather than absolute state.
  4. Add an automatic contradiction check: parse the emotion words out of the generated direction, map them to the 40-emotion vocabulary, and verify that their plane positions are monotone in the same direction as the measured window sequence. Reject and regenerate on contradiction, and record the check result in the row. This turns "the LLM might hallucinate" from a hope into a measured rejection rate you can report.
  5. Record direction_confidence = the measured plane span of the collage. A collage whose measured span is small got a direction describing a movement that may not be there; consumers can filter on it.

Why B rather than A as the headline field: the register matters. The whole vp500 corpus was generated from direction-style prompts — the existing caption column reads "(restrained and civil, the irritation tucked under the words, keeping control by effort, a small bite only on the final words)". A dataset whose target text is in the same register as the prompts that produced it is directly usable for instruction-following TTS. A dataset whose target text is "tempo: The speech has a brisk; arousal: ..." is usable for attribute prediction and little else. The reason to keep A anyway is that it costs nothing and it is the only thing that lets you audit B.

13 · What could go wrong

Nothing in this pipeline has ever been validated against human ears Not the genuineness scorer, not the vocal-burst classifier, not the emotion heads, not the speaker similarity floor, and certainly not the collage seams — which do not exist yet. This project has produced five separate cases of a scorer confidently rewarding broken audio, and section 10.1 of this document is the sixth: ranking by genuineness + burst blend picks a 0.1-second clip containing the word "Yeah" as the best angry take available. Every number on this page is a machine agreeing with another machine.
  • Seam artefacts. The 15 ms micro-fade is a judgement, not a measurement. If it is too short there will be a click at every seam; if too long, a doubling. A model trained on 10 M collages each containing 3.5 clicks will learn to generate clicks. Mitigation: a spectral discontinuity detector on the finished audio (frame-to-frame log-spectral distance at the seam vs the clip interior) and a hard reject above threshold; plus a human listening test on 200 collages before any scale run. Nothing else catches this.
  • The LLM hallucinating an arc that is not audible. Discussed in section 11 — the specific risk is amplified by the 18 % per-clip emotion recovery, because the LLM is handed labels that the audio may not support. Mitigation: arc-aware measured window statistics in the prompt, the monotonicity contradiction check, and direction_confidence as a filterable field. Also: hold out 1,000 collages, have humans rate "does the direction match what you hear" on a 5-point scale, and publish the number. If it is below 3.5, ship Option A as the primary caption instead.
  • Quality gates correlating with blandness. Measured, not speculative: the correlation between spk_sim and genuineness is −0.09 and between spk_sim and burst blend is −0.14 (Pearson, n = 1.61 M). Small correlations, but they run the wrong way and they compound across a gate. Worse, sorting a group by spk_sim descending — which is what a naive "clean seams" rule does — returns takes whose emotion argmax is "interest, concentration" for every intended emotion, i.e. it reliably selects the take where the emotion adapter did the least. Mitigation: the seam rule in section 5.3 gates on clip-to-clip similarity with a burst exemption, never on emotional intensity; and the coverage audit is by intended label, so a systematically bland stratum shows up as a stratum that cannot be filled.
  • The emotion is not actually in the audio. Section 6: per-clip 40-way recovery is 18 %. The centroids are right, so the emotion adapters do something — but if for some voice or some emotion they do nothing, we will build arcs out of clips that all sound the same and label them as transitions. Mitigation: compute per-voice, per-emotion centroid separation before building, and exclude voice × emotion cells whose separation is below a threshold. This is cheap (parquet only) and it should be done in the pilot.
  • Speaker identity drifting across a collage without any single seam failing. Each seam can pass at 0.30 while clip 1 and clip 6 are 0.05 apart. Mitigation: a global constraint — every pair of non-adjacent clips must also exceed 0.15, and the mean of all pairwise cosines must exceed 0.25. Cheap to compute (6 clips = 15 pairs).
  • The burst exemption becoming a loophole. If 6 % of collages are burst-pivot and the exemption is generous, the exempt seams become the place where all the bad audio hides. The burst classifier is already known to be suspect — 77 of 151 detected spans came back as "Low Mumble". Mitigation: the blend ≥ 4.0 requirement, the exclusive-or rule, the 0.10 residual floor, and the fact that every exempt seam is flagged so the whole class can be dropped in one filter.
  • The emotion head having an "Interest" prior rather than the corpora being flat. Section 7.3: Interest plus Concentration account for 87–95 % of every natural corpus, and for 65 % of a synthetic corpus that is constructed to be one fortieth each of 40 emotions. Those two facts together mean the argmax is partly an artefact. If it is mostly artefact, every "emotional spread" number in section 7 is understated and the natural corpora are more usable than they look; if it is mostly real, the arcs in a natural-corpus v2 do not exist to be found. Mitigation: a targeted check — take the synthetic corpus, where the intended emotion is known exactly, and measure the head's calibration per emotion. That is a parquet-only job and it settles the question for all corpora at once. It should happen before any v2 scoping.
  • Deriving speaker identity from a uid string. Section 7.1 found that the obvious Emilia rule silently destroys 54 % of the corpus, that EuroParl's key is a parliamentary session rather than a person, and that the podcast's is a channel. Each of those would have produced a confidently wrong "speakers" number. Mitigation: the within-key VoiceNet variance test against a shuffled control, applied to every derived key before it is trusted. It costs seconds and it caught three errors here.
  • Corpus totals. This risk is now retired for the five ingested corpora: they were counted exactly rather than extrapolated (section 2), and the old ±20 % band turned out to contain but not centre the truth — Emilia was 23.5 % under. Kartoffelphon and the vp500 hours remain estimates.
  • Over-building before validation. The costing shows 100 M collages is affordable. That is the trap. Producing 100 M unvalidated items makes the eventual listening test more expensive to act on, not less, because a rule change means rebuilding everything.

14 · Recommendation

Build 1 M collages, from the synthetic corpus only, at top-3 gating, with the arc-aware procedural caption and a constrained LLM direction — and run a human listening test on 200 of them before building any of the other 999,800.

Scale: 1 M. Not because of cost — 100 M costs 0.41 % of the remaining allocation — but because 1 M is where clip reuse stays at 2.1× and the coverage matrix (12 archetypes × 500 voices × 2 languages × 4 lengths × 20 collages = 960,000) is exactly filled. Every collage above that number is a rearrangement of audio already in the dataset. The 10 M scenario is a reasonable second phase after validation, if a downstream model demonstrably benefits from reuse; the 100 M scenario should not be built until someone can point at a model that needed it.

Source: synthetic only, for v1. The vp500 corpus is 20.1 M candidates across 500 voices with exact speaker identity, perfectly balanced emotion coverage (40 emotions × 4 settings × 2 languages for every voice), and full annotation already on disk. Nothing else comes close for this purpose. The natural corpora are the v2 project: they offer ~645,000 qualifying releasable speakers (34,536 of them German) against the synthetic corpus's 500, which is exactly the axis vp500 is weak on — but EuroParl needs VoiceCLAP speaker clustering before it contributes anything at all, Common Voice needs its cv_client_id promoted into the index, the Emilia YODAS half needs a per-key voice-consistency filter, and all of them need the Interest-prior question answered first. Kartoffelphon is a German-TTS ingest worth doing on its own merits (~68 GPU-h), not a collage source.

Gate: top-3 with a 4-second duration floor applied before ranking. The floor is non-negotiable — without it 12.6 % of selections are degenerate.

Seams: clip-to-clip ECAPA ≥ 0.30 or WavLM second opinion, with the class-specific burst exemption, every exemption flagged. Not the 0.40 reference floor, which would discard 66 % of the angry material to protect against a cross-speaker error that cannot occur inside a single-voice collage.

Captions: both. Arc-aware procedural caption as the auditable ground truth; constrained LLM direction as the headline field, with the monotonicity check and a confidence score.

The first concrete step

One job, small enough to run today, that produces something a human can listen to:

  1. Build 200 collages — 12 archetypes × ~17 each, spread over 40 voices, both languages, L ∈ {3,4,5,6}, from the existing vp500 parquet + tars. This is pure CPU on the login node or one small node: 200 × 3.5 CPU-s ≈ 12 CPU-minutes of work. No GPU, no allocation request.
  2. Re-annotate them — 200 items through the scorer stack is under a minute of GPU time; it can ride on any existing job.
  3. Build a listening page in the style of the existing Space pages: each collage playable, with its component boundaries marked, the seam cosines shown, the procedural caption and the LLM direction side by side, and three questions per item — (a) does this sound like one person? (b) can you hear the emotion move? (c) does the written direction match what you hear?
  4. Have humans answer them. Then, and only then, decide the thresholds. Every number in section 10 is a defensible guess derived from machine measurements; four of them (seam threshold, fade length, pause distribution, loudness window) are exactly the kind of parameter that a 20-minute listening session settles better than another week of parquet analysis.

The deliverable of step 1 is not a dataset. It is the first time anything in this pipeline has been checked against a human ear. Given that the pipeline has already produced six documented cases of a scorer rewarding broken audio, that is the highest-value hour available.

Everything on this page is reproducible from data that is on disk; the analysis scripts themselves were written to session scratch and are not durable, but each measurement states its source and method so it can be re-run.
