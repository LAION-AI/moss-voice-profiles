Best-of-N voice conversion + SIDON, costed

A one-GPU pilot of expressive best-of-8 Chatterbox voice conversion over the 500-voice corpus: what it costs after the pipeline is actually optimised, what it does to speaker identity and to expression, and what the two proposed scenarios — converting all 20.1 M existing samples, and deriving 5,500 further voice profiles by borrowing and converting — would cost in GPU-hours, node-hours and disk.

The headline is a trade, not a win. Voice conversion fixes the identity problem better and far more cheaply than the LoRA repair path does — and it halves the emotion strength of every emotion-conditioned take while doing it. The VoiceNet-dimension block survives almost untouched (−2.8%); the emotion block does not. Anything that ships from this pipeline should be labelled and filtered on that basis, not merged silently into the corpus.

1 · What was piloted

354 real corpus takes drawn from four vp500 voices spanning the whole severity range of the identity problem (8.5% to 84.6% of their takes below the floor), stratified across all seven condition blocks and both languages, and selected so that 37.0% sit below the 0.40 ECAPA floor. Mean clip length 9.72 s — identical to the corpus mean, so the cost numbers extrapolate without a length correction.

Each take was converted with 8 candidates toward a prepared reference, all 8 scored, the best kept. Three arms ran over the same 354 takes:

  • self — target is the take's own reference voice. This is voice conversion as an identity-repair path, and it is scenario 1.
  • nn — target is the acoustically nearest other voice that has a corpus. This is scenario 2, with the best match it can get.
  • far — target is a deliberately distant voice (95th-percentile distance). This is scenario 2 when the nearest match is not near.

1,062 samples, 8,496 candidates, one GH200. Every candidate was scored with the corpus's own sensors (pp_scores_fast: voiceclap blend and genuineness, BUD-E emonet, 57 VoiceNet dimensions, DNSMOS-distilled quality) so before and after are the same instrument, plus two independent speaker embedders — ECAPA-TDNN, which is what the 0.40 floor is defined on, and Orange Speaker-wavLM-tbr, which is not.

The measurement trap, and how it was avoided. The prepared reference is not the same signal as the reference the corpus generator used, so ECAPA against it is not comparable to the corpus's stored spk_sim. Every identity number below is measured against manifest_<cid>.json["ref_wav"] — the exact 16 kHz file vpgen.py used — so "above the floor" means the same thing before and after. Scoring against the prepared reference instead would have inflated the self-arm result from 64.9% to 67.8% mean ECAPA for free.

2 · Preparing the reference — and why −23 LUFS

A bad reference degrades every conversion made from it and nothing in the output says so, so the preparation chain was measured rather than trusted. All 500 references were rebuilt from <cid>.orig.mp3 — uniformly, because the shipped set is a mix of orig / sidon / cbx variants chosen per voice by DNSMOS — through SIDON restoration → EBU R128 integrated loudness → peak ceiling 0.95. 72.8 minutes of reference audio, 53.2 s of GPU (82× realtime).

The loudness target is not a matter of taste

SIDON returns audio already peak-normalised to 0.9. That leaves only +0.47 dB of headroom under a 0.95 ceiling, so any target louder than the material makes the ceiling eat the entire gain and the normalisation silently becomes a no-op. Measured over the 500 prepared references:

The project's existing reference-pool default is −18 LUFS (build_ref_pool.py). On SIDON output that target is a no-op for 92.2% of references — and the ablation below confirms it: the −18 arm and the plain peak-normalised arm produce the same conversions to three decimal places. −23 LUFS is the shallowest target that clears the ceiling for almost everything, and it is the standard EBU R128 delivery level. It collapses the reference level spread from 8.41 LU (p5–p95 of the originals) to 0.52 LU, which is the real point: 6,000 references conditioning one model should not differ by 8 dB.

SIDON on the reference is a genuine trade, not a free win. It buys quality (+0.039 Overall Q over the loudness-only arm) and noticeably better vocal-burst blend (+0.265), and it costs −0.056 ECAPA and −0.057 WavLM. Both embedders agree, so this is a real timbre shift, not an ECAPA artefact — restoring the reference moves the target slightly away from the voice as originally recorded. Across the 500 references, SIDON helped most exactly where it should: +0.151 Overall Q on the worst-quality quartile, −0.037 on the best. Identity survived (ECAPA original-vs-prepared 0.900 mean, only 0.6% of references below 0.75). The defensible rule is to loudness-normalise always and SIDON conditionally, gated on the reference's own quality score — not to restore all 6,000 blindly. The pilot ran SIDON on all of them, so its identity numbers carry that −0.06 penalty throughout.

3 · Throughput

The project has a precedent here: LeVo went from 0.67× to 11.7× realtime once it stopped being CPU-dispatch-bound. The same disease was present in three places.

Batching the generator

Voice conversion is stochastic — the flow-matching decoder starts from Gaussian noise, so the N candidates are N independent draws that can share one forward pass. Batching them is where most of the speed is:

candidates in one pass, single source
One source clip, fp32. 545 ms per candidate unbatched → 126 ms at N=64: a 4.3× saving that costs nothing but arranging the tensor.

Beyond that, several different sources can be packed into the same pass. That works, but the gain is small — once ~64 items are in flight the GH200 is saturated and it stops mattering where they came from:

sources packed per pass, N=8
N=8 throughout, length-bucketed. 137 ms/candidate at M=1 → 118 ms at M=8 (64 in flight), then flat and slightly worse. Peak memory rises 2.7 → 49 GB across this range, so M=8 is also the cheap point.
Packing is only legal if the padding does not change the audio, so that was checked rather than assumed. Sources in a packed batch have different token lengths and must be right-padded. Against the natural seed-to-seed spread as the control: output lengths are identical to the solo case; log-energy envelope correlation is 0.9775 packed-vs-solo against a control of 0.9789; BUD-E encoder cosine is 0.9981 against a control of 0.9976. Packing is indistinguishable from re-rolling the seed. Length bucketing matters more than packing itself: sorting sources by duration cuts padding waste from 51% to 8% and buys 16% throughput.

SIDON was 290× slower than it needed to be

The upstream expressive_bestofn pipeline restores candidates one at a time through the TorchScript sidon-v0.1 path. On a 16 s clip that measured 11,017 ms per candidate here, and batching it made no difference — the time was going into a CPU-side feature extractor, not the GPU. This project already solved that properly: mediathek_sidon/code/sidon_batch.py featurises per item (the stacked-frame extractor's attention mask disagrees with the real frame count for some lengths, which a naive padded batch gets wrong on 15 of 16 items), batches the w2v-BERT encoder, and groups the DAC decoder by identical frame count because that decoder is not causal. Reusing it, with the remaining CPU featurisation moved to a thread pool:

1.91× end to end, and none of it cost quality. TF32 on the flow-matching decoder is free: identical scores to four decimals (ECAPA 0.626087 vs 0.626063, Overall Q 3.0118 vs 3.0114) for a 1.60× generation speed-up. This is worth stating because this project measured TF32 costing ~15 dB in SIDON — s3gen simply does not have that sensitivity, and it was checked rather than inherited. MP3 encoding was 15% of the budget as a serial CPU tail and drops to 2.8% in a thread pool. Dropping SIDON from the output path is also a quality improvement, for reasons in §4.

Generation is now 88% of the remaining budget. The model is the bottleneck again, which is where it should be.

Converting a take to its own reference is a very effective identity repair. Of the 131 sampled takes below the floor, 95.4% cross it, mean ECAPA 0.246 → 0.553. Takes that were already above the floor stay above it and get better (0.637 → 0.705), so this is not a treatment that has to be targeted at the failures — it is safe to run over everything, which is what the owner asked for.
Borrowing another voice's corpus is a different proposition entirely. In the nn arm, takes that were already above the floor fall from 100% to 86.5%. In the far arm they fall to 61.9%, and the arm as a whole ends up no better than it started (63.0% → 62.7%). Conversion does not confer identity for free; it confers it in proportion to how close the target already was. Correlation between match distance and achieved ECAPA across the eight source/target pairs: r = −0.755.

The two floors disagree, and that matters

The 0.40 figure is an ECAPA-scale convention, not a perceptual fact. Of the pilot's converted takes that remain below the ECAPA floor, 50% are above WavLM's own same-speaker threshold. On the other side, 98.6% of converted takes clear the WavLM threshold against 98.3% clearing the ECAPA one — here the two sensors broadly agree that conversion worked, which is a stronger claim than either alone. The disagreement is concentrated in the residue.

Conversion halves the emotion. The conditioned emotion's own emonet score drops 50.8% on the emotion block, and it drops by almost exactly the same amount in all three arms (−50.5% far, −51.3% nn) — so it is conversion itself doing it, not the choice of target. The VoiceNet-dimension block, which is 456 of the 842 groups, is essentially unharmed at −2.8%: steered voice qualities such as brightness or roughness survive resynthesis, while the acted intensity of an emotional performance does not. Blend and genuineness both improve, which is consistent with a vocoder that smooths and normalises.

This is the finding that would have been missed by measuring identity alone, and it is the reason a converted corpus is not a drop-in replacement for the original.

On this corpus, best-of-8 is a poor trade. Going from 1 candidate to 8 costs 5.2× the GPU time and buys +0.57 pp above the floor, +6.2% emotion strength and +0.021 Overall Q. Most of what there is arrives by k=2 or k=4. The upstream expressive_bestofn recommendation of N=8 was derived on a different problem — converting 52 highly expressive source clips to one voice, where seed-to-seed variance is large. On converted corpus takes the candidate spread is small, because the source already fixes the content, the prosody and the timing; the decoder has little left to vary.

Both scenarios are costed at N=8 below, as asked, and at N=1, 2 and 4 — because the difference is a factor of five on a very large number.

SIDON should be dropped from the output path. On converted audio it costs quality rather than adding it: −0.056 Overall Q, −0.018 Speech Q, −0.041 ECAPA and −0.021 WavLM, buying only +0.25 blend. That is the expected result on reflection — SIDON is a restoration model for degraded recordings, and a fresh 24 kHz HiFi-GAN output is not degraded. It belongs on the reference (conditionally), not on the product. Removing it saves 4.3% of the pipeline budget and improves the result.

7 · Scenario 2 — deriving 5,500 further voices

For each voice with no corpus, find an existing profile whose reference is acoustically similar, then convert that profile's generated corpus to the new reference. There are 6,064 voice profiles in the pool and 500 have corpora, so the true remainder is 5,564; the figures below use the requested 5,500.

The matching step is free

The 99-dimensional VoiceNet annotation for all 6,064 references already exists (annotations/dims_enh.npy, verified aligned to voices.parquet row by row). Z-scoring it and taking the full dense 6,064×6,064 distance matrix takes 0.48 s of CPU; the 5,564×500 argmin takes 0.026 s in 22 MB. Against a conversion job measured in tens of thousands of GPU-hours, matching is 0% of the cost — which means every matching policy can be explored exhaustively before a single GPU is booked, and should be.

Roughly one voice in five has no real match. For 1,245 of the 5,564, the "nearest" voice with a corpus is no nearer than a voice picked at random would be one time in twenty. The pilot's far arm is what those look like: 61.9% of already-good takes fall below the floor, and the arm ends up no better than the unconverted corpus. Those voices should be excluded, not shipped.

Categorical metadata is not a substitute for this measurement: matching on gender and language alone moves the median distance only from 13.30 to 12.29, against 6.81 for a real acoustic match. And the acoustic match itself gets gender wrong 29.5% of the time (language, where both are known, agrees 97.8%; mean age difference 11.5 years). A hard gender constraint on the matcher costs nothing to add and should be added.

So 5,500 derived profiles are not 5,500 performances — they are 500 performances wearing 5,500 voices, and half of them wearing one of 50.

What it costs

5,500 × 40,256 × 8 = 1,771,264,000 candidates, from 221,408,000 samples. That is the number, plainly. It is 11× scenario 1.

Compute is not what should decide this. The 12-day wall clock and the 43 TB are more real, and the quality argument is more real still.

Keeping every candidate's audio would cost 375 TB and buys nothing: the losing candidates are only useful as scores, and those fit in 676 GB for both scenarios combined.

The proposed layout

Nothing is deleted and nothing is written into an existing run directory. Converted audio lands in a sibling tree, keyed so that provenance is recoverable from the key alone:

vprof/vp500/<voice>/PPILOT2/            # untouched, byte for byte
    cands-{000..003}.tar
    meta-{000..003}-<ts>.parquet

vprof/vc500/<voice>/VC1/                # scenario 1: converted to its own reference
    conv-{000..003}.tar                 # WebDataset: <key>.mp3 + <key>.json
    prov-{000..003}.parquet             # one row per shipped clip
    cand-{000..003}.parquet             # all 8 candidates' scores, no audio
    REF.json                            # prepared reference: sha256, lufs_pre/post, sidon flag
    VC.DONE

vprof/vcderived/<new_voice>/VC1/        # scenario 2: borrowed from another voice
    ... same shape, plus source_voice and match_distance in REF.json

key = <target_voice>/<source_voice>/<gid with | as __>.c<NNN>.k<J>

Four properties this buys:

  1. The join key is (run_dir, audio_key), never audio_key alone. audio_key is not unique across runs — this project has already served the wrong run's audio to a page once because of it. prov-*.parquet stores source_run_dir as a column, which the corpus parquet does not, so a converted row can always be traced back to exactly one original take.
  2. The WebDataset key is self-describing. Target voice, source voice, group, source candidate and conversion candidate index are all recoverable from the member name, so a tar that has drifted away from its parquet is still interpretable.
  3. Per-clip provenance travels with the audio in the .json sidecar: source run and key, target voice, candidate index, rank, and every score used to rank it, plus ECAPA and WavLM against both the prepared and the original reference.
  4. Selection is a view, not a copy. One manifest parquet lists (variant, voice, run_dir, key) for original and converted alike. A consumer that wants "the original unless it was below the floor, then the converted one" expresses that as a filter over scores that are already in the table, and the answer changes without moving a byte. Old and new coexist permanently and no policy decision is baked into the storage.

The reference itself is versioned: REF.json records the sha256 of the prepared reference, its measured loudness before and after, and whether SIDON was applied. If the SIDON rule changes, the affected corpora are identifiable without re-listening.

9 · Versus the LoRA identity repair

A separate effort attacks the same problem by training a rank-4 identity LoRA per voice and regenerating below-floor takes. Both were measured with the same ECAPA sensor and the same floor:

Voice conversion reaches a higher floor-crossing rate on all 20.1 M takes for roughly one-eighth of the GPU cost the LoRA path needs for the 8.2 M failures alone, at a comparable expression cost and with better blend and genuineness. It also needs no per-voice training run, no held-out group management, and no per-voice adapter to ship and version.

The two are not equivalent, though, and the difference is not in the numbers. The LoRA regenerates the take: the result is a new performance by a model that has been nudged toward the target identity, and it is still a synthesis of that voice. Conversion resynthesises an existing take's content through a different timbre — the performance is the old one, the voice is new. For scenario 1 those converge, because the target identity is the take's own. The comparison here is between two costs paid for the same repair; it is not evidence that conversion is the better artistic object. The caveats are symmetric: both were measured on the sensor that defines the problem.

10 · Recommendation

Do scenario 1. Do not do scenario 2 as specified.

Scenario 1 — run it, at N=4, without SIDON on the output. 2,730 GPU-hours, 683 node-hours, 0.19% of the remainder, about 14 hours on 48 nodes, 3.9 TB. It takes the whole corpus from 63% above the identity floor to ~98%, improves the takes that were already fine, needs no per-voice training, and costs a third of what the LoRA path costs for a subset of the same job. N=4 rather than N=8 because best-of-8 buys +0.6 pp above the floor and +6% emotion strength for 5.2× the compute; if the extra 0.02 Overall Q matters, N=8 is still only 0.34% of the remainder.

Ship it beside the original, never over it, with the per-clip provenance and the score-driven view in §8, and label every converted clip as expression-attenuated. The corpus then contains two versions of every take and the choice between them is a filter, made downstream, reversibly.

Scenario 2 — do not run it at 5,500 voices. The compute is affordable (3.8% of the remainder at N=8, 0.73% at N=1) and that is not the objection. The objections are:

  • 1,245 of the 5,564 have no real acoustic match. The pilot's far arm shows what those produce: an arm that ends up no better than the unconverted corpus, with 38% of previously-good takes falling below the floor.
  • The derived corpora are not new performances. Prosody, timing and acting choices come from the source voice. Half of the 5,500 would descend from 50 seeds. Calling the result "5,500 voice profiles" would overstate what the dataset contains by a large factor.
  • The emotion attenuation compounds. A derived corpus is already 50% down on emotion strength and is a different speaker's performance. For the emotion block — 320 of 842 groups — that is close to losing the condition.

The version worth running instead: restrict to the borrowers with a genuinely close match, add a hard gender constraint, and cap fan-out so no seed is reused more than ~20 times. Taking the 49.0% whose match is at or better than the gold p75 gives ~2,700 voices at 14,719 GPU-hours at N=4 (0.99% of the remainder, 21 TB) and a defensible claim about every one of them. That is a much better dataset than 5,500 voices of which a fifth are arbitrary.

Risks

  • The 0.40 floor is the sensor this is optimised against, and it is a convention. An independent WavLM embedder already disagrees with it on three-quarters of the corpus's selected "failures". Conversion is being scored on the metric that defines the problem, which is circular. The WavLM numbers here agree that conversion works, which is reassuring but not independent validation — neither embedder is a listener. A human listening test on a few hundred pairs is the missing evidence, and it is cheap next to 5,000 GPU-hours.
  • The expression loss is measured on the same emonet that steered the generation. A −51% emonet score is strong evidence of attenuation but is not calibrated to perception; the true perceptual loss could be larger or smaller.
  • Four voices, 354 takes. The pilot spans the severity range well but the per-block and per-arm cells are small (7–12 takes for explicit, sports, edge). The headline self-arm result rests on 131 below-floor takes. Widen to ~20 voices before committing 5,000 GPU-hours.
  • Conversion inherits the source take's defects. If a take is unintelligible or truncated, the converted take is unintelligible or truncated in a new voice. Nothing in the reward catches this — WER was not re-measured in the pilot. Add an ASR gate before shipping.
  • Non-speech takes are not helped and may be harmed. Isolated vocal bursts reach only 87.5% above floor and 2 of the 11 listenable examples are outright failures. The burst_isolated and non-speech edge groups should be excluded from conversion rather than shipped weak.
  • SIDON on the reference costs 0.06 ECAPA. The pilot applied it to all 500. The conditional rule (restore only low-quality references) is supported by the data here but has not itself been run end to end.
  • Storage. 47 TB is a filesystem decision, not a compute one, and the scenario-2 share of it (43 TB) is being spent on the least defensible half of the plan.

11 · What is not verified here

  • No human listening test. Every quality claim is model-scored.
  • WER / intelligibility of converted takes was not measured.
  • The LoRA comparison is against that pipeline's published aggregate over 93 voices, not a matched re-run on the same 4 voices and 131 takes. It is indicative, not a controlled comparison.
  • Scenario-2 quality is measured on 4 source voices with one nearest and one distant target each — 8 pairs. The correlation of −0.755 between distance and achieved identity is suggestive at that n, not established.
  • The cost model assumes the measured 9.72 s mean clip and linear scaling in sample count. It does not include tar/parquet write bandwidth at 47 TB, decode of the source corpus from its tars, or job scheduling overhead — on the pilot, source decode was under 1% of wall time, but at scale the read side of 3.9 TB is not free.
  • Multi-GPU and multi-node scaling was not measured. Node-hours assume perfect 4-GPU packing.
