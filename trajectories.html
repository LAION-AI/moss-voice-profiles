Trajectory collages — VoiceNet ladders and emotion arcs

40 collages, each a single voice moving through a progression, spliced into one clip. 160 kbps.

What you are listening to. A trajectory is not one take — it is several takes of the same voice at rising or falling levels of one dimension, spliced together so the progression is audible in a single listen. A vulnerability ladder goes extremely low → moderately low → moderately high → very high. The movement is the content.
The three columns answer different questions.
raw — the spliced collage as generated.
+ Chatterbox VC — voice conversion toward that voice's own reference. Changes who it sounds like.
+ Sidon — speech restoration, 48 kHz, loudness normalised. Changes how clean it sounds, not who.
A caveat that matters more here than anywhere else. A trajectory is spliced, so it inherits the seam problem — and per-part voice conversion does not fix fragmentation, because each part is converted without knowledge of its neighbours. Worse, for a trajectory the rung boundaries are the content: a conversion pass that smooths them away would defeat the purpose of the clip. Listen for whether the ladder is still audible after conversion, not just whether the voice is steadier.

How to judge these

  • Is the progression audible at all? That is the whole purpose. spread is the measured distance between the first and last rung; monotonic means every step moved in the intended direction. A high spread that you cannot hear is a scorer artefact worth reporting.
  • Are the joins audible? Separate question from the progression. A trajectory should change because the rung changed, not because a splice happened.
  • After conversion, is the ladder still there? If VC flattens the progression it has traded away the reason the clip exists.
No human has scored any of this. Spread and monotonicity come from automatic VoiceNet heads whose agreement with a listener on spoken material is about ρ ≈ +0.21. Use them to find interesting clips, not as verdicts.
